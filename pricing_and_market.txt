Commercial Space Tourism: Pricing and Market Data

Suborbital tickets are the most accessible tier of space tourism. Orbital Horizon Aerospace has publicly listed suborbital seat prices starting around 450,000 dollars, though early passengers on founder-era flights paid substantially more. Aurelia Spaceworks has sold suborbital seats for approximately 250,000 to 600,000 dollars depending on the era of sale, with the lowest prices reserved for deposit holders who reserved seats more than a decade before flying.

Halcyon Voyages prices its stratospheric balloon flights lower than either rocket-based suborbital competitor, with published fares around 125,000 dollars per seat, reflecting the lower cost and lower risk profile of balloon-based flight compared to rocket propulsion.

Orbital tourism remains far more expensive. Zenith Orbital Systems has arranged private astronaut missions to the International Space Station with reported per-seat costs in the range of 50 to 55 million dollars, covering seat cost, training, and mission support.

Industry analysts estimate that fewer than a thousand paying passengers have flown on suborbital space tourism flights since the sector's first commercial flights began in the early 2020s.
