Commercial Space Tourism: Safety Record and Regulation

Aurelia Spaceworks experienced two fatal accidents during its flight-test program, in 2014 and 2016. The 2014 accident involved the premature deployment of the vehicle's re-entry feathering system, resulting in the death of one test pilot and serious injury to a second. Following each accident, Aurelia grounded its test program for extended investigation and redesign before resuming flights.

Orbital Horizon Aerospace has not reported any fatalities among crew or passengers on its suborbital vehicle, though an earlier uncrewed test vehicle was lost in a 2015 flight-test accident that the company attributed to an engine failure. All crewed flights since Orbital Horizon began carrying passengers have completed successfully.

Passengers on suborbital and orbital tourism flights are typically required to sign informed-consent waivers acknowledging the experimental nature of commercial spaceflight and the associated risks, in addition to passing medical screening appropriate to their planned flight profile.
